Brochure or funnel: which company website fits your budget

Author: Marcin Bochenek · Updated: 2026-08-24

Most disappointment is not an ugly layout. It is buying a brochure when you needed a funnel — or a funnel when a form would do. I split it hard: the Start package from PLN 2,000 versus the Launch package from PLN 8,000. Criteria below, not a personality quiz.

A brochure: a site for people already walking toward you

A brochure works when the source is a referral, LinkedIn, a Google Business Profile, or a trade where the client will find you anyway. Its job: confirm you are serious, show scope, give one way to contact. That is a B2B company website of up to five pages, not a sales engine.

I do not promise it “ranks you first”. I promise that after someone types your name, they see an offer, not a parking page or a 2014 Facebook. If your name is not searched at all, fix the channel before the button colour.

  • One CTA: a form or a 20-minute audit.
  • A price or a range in sight.
  • Proof you can click — or an honest absence, not fake stars.
  • Mobile that does not lose the submit field.

A funnel: a site that must earn back the cost of a click

A funnel starts when you pay for attention or the decision needs more than “send an email”. Qualification (how many apartments, what invoice volume, what budget), a landing for a specific campaign, measurement from click to enquiry — that is the Launch package (from PLN 8,000). Booking on your domain is already Platform (from PLN 25,000), not an “extended brochure”.

A live example: Mint Apartments. The guest compares the price with Booking.com. If the site only tells stories, the booking still lands on the portal. The funnel is a live calendar, a cheaper night on your domain, a lock code. You cannot stuff that into PLN 8,000 without lying about scope.

I do not have a public “+347% conversion” case. I have a working site and a mechanic you can click. If someone sells a funnel with percentages and no URL, that is a slide.

If three rows say “funnel” and the budget is 10k, I do not build a “mini-funnel”. I say we do not ship, or we wait until the numbers close. A mini-funnel is usually a brochure with hidden costs.

The usual mistake: buy a funnel with your eyes, pay for a brochure

Brief: “we want booking, AI, a blog, three languages and a shop, budget PLN 9,000”. That is not an ambitious client. That is an incoherent scope. Either we cut to a Start brochure and plan the expansion, or we go Platform (from PLN 25,000). The blend ends as a site that pretends everything and measures nothing.

  1. Name one conversion: enquiry / booking / call.
  2. Check whether you can measure it a week after launch.
  3. Only then add languages, a blog, an assistant.

A guest assistant and HITL are a separate layer — not brochure decoration. I cover that in implementing ChatGPT. On a company site, a human on the other side of the form is enough.

When a brochure is the right choice, not a shameful one

A small law firm, a workshop, a service sole trader, a freelancer living on referrals: a brochure is a professional tool, not a failure. Shame is faking a funnel without budget for copy, measurement and follow-up. I prefer five clear pages to a “platform” nobody can run after handover.

I plan the expansion from day one: same stack, same URLs, no rewrite next year because “now we want booking”. If you know paid traffic is two seasons away, I say so at the start — even if you pay for a brochure today.

How we close this in 20 minutes

I do not run a persona workshop. I ask about enquiry sources, acquisition cost, what happens after the email, and whether the calendar lives in Excel. Then one recommendation. Money detail: what a company website costs. Funnel detail: the next article in this cluster.

A paper test: whether a brochure has a right to pay back

A brochure makes sense when traffic is warm: referral, LinkedIn, a conversation you already have. The site confirms the offer, a range, proof, and one way to get in touch. It does not need to qualify volume or close payment. If all you need is “somewhere to stand when someone searches the company name” — the Start package (from PLN 2,000) is the right box. Paid traffic onto that site with no path burns budget. Then we do not “extend the brochure”. We change product to a funnel.

A funnel starts when you pay for attention or the decision needs more than an email: what invoice volume, what budget, a campaign landing, measurement from click to enquiry — that is the Launch package, from PLN 8,000. Booking on your domain is already Platform, from PLN 25,000, and another calendar. What a company site costs will not grow into a funnel by “adding a section”. That is another contract.

  • Brochure: one language, up to 5 pages, a form, on-page, no CRM in price.
  • Funnel: qualification, maybe payment/booking, landings, measurement, often a second language.
  • Panel: when Excel breaks the process — that is no longer a brochure/funnel dilemma.

The usual buying error: a funnel at brochure price

“Let’s make a company site, and while we are at it booking, AI and a blog”. While-we-are-at-it is a separate project. If the brief mixes those, a 20-minute audit may end in a stop or split invoices. I would rather lose the deal than pretend a Start (PLN 2k) or Launch (PLN 8k) budget covers a Platform build with booking. The market is full of sites that “do not sell” because someone bought wallpaper for a funnel’s job. Why a site does not sell often starts with that mix-up, not the button colour.

How we decide on a call, without a moodboard

I ask where conversations come from today and what happens to them. I ask whether anyone already pays for a click. I ask whether conversion is mail, a booking, or “leave a number”. If you cannot name that, we do not pick a funnel “just in case”. We pick a brochure or nothing. Nothing is a legal option. It saves 25k on a path you will not staff with follow-up. A funnel on a dead inbox only accelerates embarrassment.

Mint is a live booking funnel on its own domain, not a brochure with a gallery. Plumm is an operations product. I do not mash them into one “universal company-site case”.

The sentence we write down at the end of 20 minutes

“A brochure, because traffic is warm and conversion is mail.” Or: “A funnel, because you pay for clicks and must qualify.” Or: “Nothing, because there is no follow-up.” That sentence is the product of the call. The rest is a moodboard. I do not stretch a brochure into a funnel with a section. I do not sell a funnel at 8k. Mint is a booking funnel. Plumm is not a brochure case. If the brief mixes booking, AI and a blog “on the side”, we split invoices or stop. I would rather lose the deal than add to the statistic of sites that do not sell because the wrong box was bought.

Questions

Can I start with a brochure and “add a funnel later”?

Yes, if the stack and IA allow it. I plan that in the audit. I do not bolt a funnel onto a WordPress theme I will not maintain.

Does a funnel always mean Google Ads?

No. A funnel is the path on the site: qualification, booking, measurement. Ads are fuel, not the definition.

I have PLN 15,000. What do we pick?

Usually a Start brochure with room for copy and measurement — or we wait for a Launch/Platform budget. 15k of a “half funnel” is the worst compromise.

Is Mint a brochure?

No. It is a service with on-domain booking and guest operations. Different package, different price, different goal.

What paper test splits a brochure from a funnel?

Where conversations come from today and what happens to them. Whether anyone pays for a click. Whether conversion is mail, a booking or “leave a number”. Warm traffic and one form → the Start brochure (from PLN 2,000). Paid attention, volume qualification → the Launch funnel (from PLN 8,000). Booking on the domain → Platform (from PLN 25,000). Cannot name conversion → nothing or a brochure, not a funnel just in case. Nothing saves the Launch budget on a path you will not staff with follow-up. Excel that breaks a process is already a panel dilemma, not this pair.

Why refuse a “company site with booking and AI while we are at it”?

While-we-are-at-it is a separate project and a separate invoice. The Launch budget (from PLN 8,000) does not cover the Platform package (from PLN 25,000) with booking. The market is full of wallpaper bought for a funnel’s job, after which we blame the button. A 20-minute audit may split scope or say stop. I would rather lose the deal than fake one product. Mint is a booking funnel on the domain — that is Platform. Plumm is an operations product. I do not glue them into a universal brochure-with-chat case.

One package recommendation, not three quotes

In the audit I will say: Start, Launch, Platform, or wait. Ranges are in pricing.
