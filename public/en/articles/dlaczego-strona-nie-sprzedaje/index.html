Why a website does not sell: three buckets before you touch the layout

Author: Marcin Bochenek · Updated: 2026-08-24

“Make it prettier” is the most expensive hypothesis you can buy when a site does not sell. Before anyone opens Figma I split the problem into three buckets: the wrong traffic, the wrong path, the wrong handling after the click. Below is the procedure I use in the audit — not a recycled “12 UX sins” list and not invented case studies.

Bucket 1: traffic that has no right to convert

If nobody arrives, the site has nothing to sell. That is not a layout problem. That is the channel: no name in Google, no referrals, a campaign on a query that pulls students, not owners. A redesign is theatre. First the source: where the last ten enquiries came from (if any), the cost per click, the query.

Second variant: there is traffic, but the wrong kind. A “trends 2026” blog pulls the curious. A B2B offer needs budget and pain. You measure sessions, not enquiries — and conclude the “site does not sell”. It is badly aimed content, not the button. That is why a B2B company website in my brochure package does not include an “SEO blog”.

I do not quote an “average B2B CR from report X”; without your ICP it is decoration. In the audit I count your enquiries and your handling cost.

Bucket 2: a path that never reaches a decision

Warm traffic, empty inbox. Causes I actually see: an H1 about the company instead of the decision; no order of magnitude on price; five CTAs; a nine-field form; mailto on mobile; no proof — or worse, fake reviews that collapse when someone checks. I do not invent testimonials. If you have no live URL, we write process and terms.

  • Is it clear in ten seconds who this is for?
  • Can you send an enquiry with a thumb, without pinch-zoom?
  • After submit, does someone get mail within a minute, not “we will check on Friday”?
  • Do you measure submits, or only pageviews?

This is often where you need a funnel, not a new palette. Or the opposite: a funnel is overkill and you lack one clear offer. Brochure versus funnel is a separate budget decision.

Bucket 3: follow-up a website cannot win

The form arrives, then silence: a PDF quote a week later, the salesperson on leave, an Excel of “new leads” nobody touches. The site “does not sell” because sales does not exist as a process. That is the edge of an operations panel, or at least notifications and a reply SLA. I do not treat that with a button gradient.

At Mint the problem was not an “ugly site”. It was portal commission and hours on guest questions. The site started selling when a guest could book cheaper on the operator’s domain. At Plumm — when a sole trader stopped hopping between Excel and an accountant’s office. That is operations wired to an interface, not UX magic.

What I refuse when you ask to “boost sales”

  1. I do not add fake testimonials.
  2. I do not promise a Google rank in 30 days.
  3. I do not redraw everything if analytics show 80% of drop-off is LCP or the wrong channel.
  4. I do not drop an AI chat on a brochure to “liven up” a missing offer.

If after twenty minutes the ROI does not close, I say so. The audit exists so you do not buy a build out of habit.

How I prove which bucket it is

I need: Search Console or at least enquiry sources for 90 days, a mobile recording (or I open it on a phone), the mail path after the form, reply time. Without that we guess. With it we can tell “nobody comes” from “they come and leave” from “they come, write, and die in the inbox”.

Speed sits in bucket 2 but earns its own article: website speed and SEO. INP and LCP on a phone can kill paid traffic before the H1 promises anything. Do not confuse that with “we need more animation”.

After the diagnosis — one change, not twelve

There is one leading job: channel, path or follow-up. We ship that, measure two weeks, then add the next. Owners love 40-point checklists because they look like work. I like one metric: qualified enquiries or time to first reply. The rest is commentary.

Bucket 1: nobody comes, and you blame the button

Why a website does not sell — traffic first. If there are no enquiries, check Search Console or at least the inbox: does anyone type the company name, or does a “trends 2026” blog pull the curious with no budget. B2B does not live on sessions. It lives on people who can buy. A brochure with no channel (referral, LinkedIn, paid) is not “failing to sell”. It has nobody to serve. A redesign will not fix that. A source will, or a decision that the site confirms a conversation rather than acting as a magnet.

A second variant of bucket 1: traffic exists, but it is the wrong kind. Then the H1 promises something other than the offer. Or the campaign target is wider than you can serve. You measure sessions, not enquiries, and conclude the “site does not sell”. Badly aimed content sells. That is why the company-site package has no SEO blog. A blog without a cluster and crawlable URLs is a hobby, not a funnel.

Buckets 2 and 3: they arrive and leave, or they write and die

Bucket 2 is INP, LCP, a first screen with no offer, five CTAs, no price, no proof. The guest cannot tell in five seconds on a phone whether this is for them. Speed is a sales tool here, not Lighthouse decoration. Bucket 3 is follow-up: the form works, mail dies, reply SLA is three days. Then a new layout is wallpaper. You need discipline or a ticket panel, not purple.

  1. A mobile recording — or I open it on my phone.
  2. The mail path after submit: who, when, which template.
  3. Time to first reply on the last 20 enquiries, not “usually fast”.
  4. Whether anyone knows which enquiry is junk and which is budget.

Without that we guess. With it we can say: they do not come / they come and leave / they come, write, and die. Three different invoices. One “make it prettier” is the most expensive guess I know. Audit Sprint from PLN 1,500 exists to split those before anyone buys 40k of layout.

What I do when someone wants “just a new layout”

I ask for three things before I open Figma: enquiry sources, a mobile recording, reply time. If they are missing, I say outright you are buying wallpaper. Sometimes wallpaper is fine — brand, shame about the old site, warm referral traffic. Then a brochure. Sometimes wallpaper is more expensive than the truth: you pay for Ads onto a heavy hero while mail sits three days. Then layout is the enemy. You do not need me to pick a font. You need a bucket. Audit Sprint costs less than a rebrand that misses the bucket.

I do not publish magic percentages of “sites that started selling after a redesign”. Mint and Plumm exist so you can click a working path, not so I can pretend every business grows after a new H1. If after diagnosis you hear “do not ship”, take it as a saving. The market will gladly sell you prettier. I would rather name which of the three reasons kills sales — and which package even has a right to pay back.

Questions

The site looks good and there are no enquiries. Is it SEO?

Not necessarily. I first check whether there is traffic and what kind. SEO can be guilty, but so can the offer or mailto on mobile.

Will a new layout raise sales?

Only if bucket 2 is the bottleneck and the layout truly blocks the decision. A “modern look” without changing CTA, price and measurement usually does nothing.

I need testimonials on the site. Can we add them?

Only real ones, with a name the client accepts, or a link to a live deployment. I will not write reviews for you.

How long does this diagnosis take?

Twenty minutes to start. If we must go into analytics and process — Audit Sprint from PLN 1,500, creditable toward a build when it makes sense.

How do I know which of the three buckets I am in?

Without Search Console, a mobile recording and the mail path you guess. Bucket 1: no traffic or the wrong traffic — a redesign will not help. Bucket 2: they arrive and leave because of the first screen, INP or a missing offer. Bucket 3: they write and die in the inbox. Three different invoices. “Make it prettier” is the most expensive guess. Audit Sprint exists to split those before anyone buys 40k of layout.

Will more blog posts fix the wrong traffic?

A trends blog pulls the curious with no budget. B2B lives on people who can buy. The company-site package has no SEO blog, because a hobby is not a funnel. If you want an organic channel, we plan clusters and crawlable URLs like this journal — not a hash in an SPA and not a “blog” plugin in a page builder. Otherwise you will measure sessions and conclude again that the site does not sell.

Will a new layout fix unanswered enquiries?

No. That is bucket 3. The form may work while mail dies or SLA is three days. Purple will not help. You need an inbox owner, a reply template and time. Sometimes a ticket panel. I do not sell wallpaper for broken follow-up. Mint and Plumm show working paths, not magic percentages of “sites that sell after a rebrand”.

Name the bucket before you buy a redesign

Twenty minutes is enough to say: channel, path or follow-up. Then, if needed, the scope of work.
